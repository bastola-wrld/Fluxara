## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The global regulatory environment for methane monitoring, reporting, and verification has undergone a structural transformation between 2022 and 2026. What was voluntary and discretionary three years ago is now mandatory, financial-penalty-backed, and expanding rapidly in geographic scope. At least eight major binding frameworks across the US, EU, Canada, and the UNFCCC now create direct, quantifiable compliance obligations that Fluxara addresses. The common constraint across all of these frameworks is the same: they require measurement-based MRV at the facility level, but no commercial product currently provides real-time, satellite-derived, facility-level attribution suitable for regulatory submission. Fluxara is designed to close that gap.

This document provides a complete regulatory landscape assessment: what each regulation requires, who it applies to, when it takes effect, what the financial consequences of non-compliance are, and how Fluxara specifically addresses the requirement.

## 2. REGULATION 1 — EU METHANE REGULATION (REGULATION (EU) 2024/1787)

### 2.1 Identity and Legal Basis

Full name: Regulation (EU) 2024/1787 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 13 June 2024 on the reduction of methane emissions in the energy sector and amending Regulation (EU) 2019/942.

The Regulation entered into force on 4 August 2024. It is directly applicable in all 27 EU Member States without requiring national transposition. The EEA countries — Iceland, Liechtenstein, and Norway — are subject to incorporation into the EEA Agreement through the EEA Joint Committee.

### 2.2 Scope

The Regulation applies to EU operators in oil and gas exploration and production (onshore and offshore), gas gathering and processing, gas transmission, distribution, underground storage, and LNG operations. An "operator" is defined as any natural or legal person who operates or controls an asset, or to whom decisive economic power over the technical functioning of an asset has been delegated.

The Regulation also impacts EU importers, as it applies to methane emissions occurring outside the Union with respect to crude oil, natural gas, and coal placed on the Union market.

### 2.3 MRV Requirements

The Regulation establishes a mandatory, asset-level Monitoring, Reporting and Verification system modelled on OGMP 2.0 standards, phased in as follows:

EU-based operators must prepare MRV procedures corresponding to OGMP 2.0 reporting standards, with the aim of achieving OGMP 2.0 Level 5 by February 2027 for operated assets, and by August 2028 for non-operated assets.

Article 12 specifies that operators must report source-level methane emissions with data per detailed emission source type, reported in tonnes of methane and in tonnes of CO2 equivalent, along with detailed information on quantification methodologies, data on share of ownership, and a list of entities with operational control.

Key milestone obligations include:

- **By 5 May 2025 and annually thereafter:** EU importers must provide national competent authorities with information on their suppliers' methane monitoring and mitigation measures
- **5 August 2025:** Annual reporting on source-level methane emissions due from operators
- **5 February 2026:** Prohibition on routine venting and flaring comes into effect
- **5 May 2025 (LDAR):** Leak detection and repair programme submission required
- **1 January 2027:** Importers must demonstrate that new supply contracts concluded since 4 August 2024 include EU-equivalent MRV at producer level
- **5 August 2028:** Importers must report methane intensity of imports annually
- **5 August 2030:** Maximum methane intensity values take effect for imported fuels

In 2026, the European Commission is expected to establish a methane transparency database, a methane monitoring tool based on satellite data, and a rapid reaction mechanism to address methane super-emitting events.

### 2.4 Financial Consequences of Non-Compliance

Significant penalties for non-compliance may apply, including fines of up to 20% of annual turnover for the relevant entity in the preceding business year. Penalties may be triggered by failure of operators to submit methane emission reports, failure of importers to provide required information, and failure of producers or importers to comply with maximum methane intensity values.

Although penalties have been voluntarily suspended for certain categories pending full implementation infrastructure, importers risk non-compliance from 1 January 2027. Potential pricing divergence may emerge between "EU Methane Regulation-compliant" and non-compliant supply as the market prices in MRV quality.

### 2.5 How Fluxara Addresses This Regulation

The EU Methane Regulation's core requirement is source- and site-level measurement-based MRV at OGMP 2.0 Level 5. Fluxara's satellite attribution pipeline produces exactly this: emission rate attributions in kg/hr at the facility level, derived from measurement (TROPOMI satellite observations), verified by independent Bayesian uncertainty quantification. The Commission's explicit plan to establish a satellite monitoring tool in 2026 validates Fluxara's approach and signals the regulatory direction of travel. Fluxara's output reports are structured to map directly to the Article 12 reporting requirements: source-type attribution, quantification methodology documentation, uncertainty bounds, and independent verification statements.

## 3. REGULATION 2 — US INFLATION REDUCTION ACT WASTE EMISSIONS CHARGE (CLEAN AIR ACT SECTION 136)

### 3.1 Identity and Legal Basis

The Waste Emissions Charge (WEC), established by Section 136 of the Clean Air Act as added by Section 60113 of the Inflation Reduction Act of 2022 (P.L. 117-169), creates the first direct federal charge on greenhouse gas emissions in US history. The charge applies to methane emissions from petroleum and natural gas facilities required to report under the EPA Greenhouse Gas Reporting Program (GHGRP), specifically Subpart W reporters.

### 3.2 The Charge Rates

The Waste Emissions Charge starts at $900 per metric ton of methane and increases to $1,500 after two years. These values equate to $36 and $60 per metric ton of CO2 equivalent respectively. The charge is calculated as the product of methane emissions reported in metric tons under the GHGRP and the applicable per-ton rate.

The original IRA schedule was: $900/tonne CH4 in 2024, $1,200 in 2025, $1,500 in 2026 and permanently thereafter.

### 3.3 Current Legal Status (Critical Update as of Q2 2026)

EPA's 2024 WEC final rulemaking received attention under the Congressional Review Act. In February 2025, both the House and Senate passed a joint resolution (H.J.Res. 35) disapproving of EPA's WEC rule. President Trump signed the measure on 14 March 2025 (P.L. 119-2). Therefore, EPA's 2024 final rule did not take effect. P.L. 119-2 changed the starting year of the WEC from 2024 to 2034. Therefore, the first WEC rate of charge is scheduled to be $1,500 in 2034.

On 12 May 2025, in line with the Congressional resolution disapproving the final WEC rule, EPA issued a final rule removing the WEC regulations from the Code of Federal Regulations.

**Assessment for Fluxara:** The WEC has been suspended until 2034 by the current US administration, removing its immediate financial driver. However, the underlying statutory authority in CAA Section 136 remains on the books and has not been repealed — only the implementing rule was disapproved. A change in administration after 2028 could reinstate the WEC implementation on the original or revised schedule. At $1,500/tonne of methane, a facility emitting 1,000 tonnes of methane per year above the GHGRP threshold would face $1.5 million annually in WEC liability. Fluxara's pipeline enables operators to identify and attribute emissions accurately, enabling precise liability calculation when the WEC is eventually enforced. The commercial relevance of the WEC to Fluxara's US market is a long-term rather than near-term driver under current political conditions.

## 4. REGULATION 3 — UNFCCC PARIS AGREEMENT ENHANCED TRANSPARENCY FRAMEWORK

### 4.1 Identity and Legal Basis

Article 13 of the Paris Agreement establishes the Enhanced Transparency Framework (ETF) for action and support, operationalised through Decision 18/CMA.1 (the Modalities, Procedures and Guidelines, or MPGs, adopted at COP24 in Katowice, 2018). The ETF supersedes the previous Measurement, Reporting and Verification (MRV) arrangements for developed countries (IAR) and developing countries (ICA) under the UNFCCC Convention.

### 4.2 Scope and Requirements

Under the Enhanced Transparency Framework, Parties to the Paris Agreement are required to submit biennial transparency reports (BTRs) every two years, with the first submission due by 31 December 2024. BTRs include information on national inventory reports, progress towards NDCs, policies and measures, climate change impacts and adaptation, and levels of financial, technology development, and capacity-building support received.

The latest reporting year included in greenhouse gas inventories must be two years prior to the submission of the report. A BTR submitted in 2024 would include data through 2022. Developing country Parties with capacity constraints may report data from three years prior to submission.

The critical structural problem for the UNFCCC framework that Fluxara addresses is this data lag. National inventories report emissions that are 2–3 years old at time of submission. Satellite-based attribution systems like Fluxara can provide near-real-time verification of national inventory data against observed atmospheric concentrations, identifying misreporting or discrepancies before they propagate into biennial submissions.

### 4.3 Article 6 Carbon Market MRV

Article 6 of the Paris Agreement creates a framework for international carbon markets, including bilateral Article 6.2 cooperative approaches and the multilateral Article 6.4 mechanism. Both require robust MRV of emission reductions that are traded as internationally transferred mitigation outcomes (ITMOs). The Paris Agreement Rules Book requires that ITMOs be real, verified, and additional. Satellite-derived methane attribution provides an independent verification layer for oil and gas emission reductions traded under Article 6, addressing the double-counting risk that currently limits market integrity.

### 4.4 How Fluxara Addresses This Framework

Fluxara's near-real-time attribution provides the measurement layer that national inventory compilers currently lack: independently verifiable, continuous, satellite-derived facility-level emission data that can be cross-checked against GHGRP/E-PRTR reported values prior to BTR submission. This creates commercial demand from national environment ministries and intergovernmental organisations (UNEP IMEO, World Bank, GEF) as customers for Fluxara's government reporting support product tier.

## 5. REGULATION 4 — EU CORPORATE SUSTAINABILITY REPORTING DIRECTIVE (DIRECTIVE (EU) 2022/2464)

### 5.1 Identity and Legal Basis

The Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD), Directive (EU) 2022/2464, entered into force on 5 January 2023, amending the Non-Financial Reporting Directive (NFRD). The CSRD is operationalised through the European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS), adopted as a delegated regulation by the European Commission on 31 July 2023. The Omnibus I simplification package, agreed in political trilogue in December 2025 and undergoing final legal adoption in early 2026, substantially modified CSRD scope and timelines.

### 5.2 Current Scope (Post-Omnibus)

The Omnibus substantially reduces the number of companies required to comply with CSRD. Under the new regulations, only EU companies with more than 1,000 employees and net annual turnover above €450 million will need to report on social and environmental matters. Non-EU companies with more than €450 million turnover generated in the EU are also in scope.

In-scope EU companies will start reporting from financial years starting on or after 1 January 2027, while non-EU companies will start reporting on or after 1 January 2028.

Wave 1 companies (large listed entities already covered by NFRD) continue reporting: they filed their first CSRD-compliant reports in 2025 covering FY 2024 data.

### 5.3 Methane Disclosure Requirements Under ESRS E1

The climate standard ESRS E1 requires companies to disclose Scope 1, 2, and 3 greenhouse gas emissions in gross tonnes, disaggregated by gas, using GHG Protocol methodologies. Methane is a Scope 1 emission for oil and gas operators and must be reported separately in tonnes of CH4 and in CO2-equivalent at both GWP-20 and GWP-100.

Companies must report gross Scope 1, 2, and 3 emissions separately from any carbon credits or removals — no netting allowed — and demonstrate a credible 1.5°C-aligned transition plan with interim targets tied to capital allocation. ESRS E1 climate disclosures are almost always material and are the most technically demanding standard to implement.

### 5.4 Financial Consequences

CSRD violations are subject to national enforcement mechanisms. In most EU member states, these include administrative penalties per unreported or inaccurate disclosure, public naming and shaming procedures, and reputational damage with capital market consequences. The sustainability report must be independently assured (limited assurance initially, reasonable assurance for larger companies over time). Inaccurate methane disclosure that misrepresents Scope 1 emissions exposes the reporting entity to assurance qualification and potential securities law liability in jurisdictions where the sustainability report forms part of the annual report.

### 5.5 How Fluxara Addresses This Regulation

CSRD's ESRS E1 methane disclosure requirements create demand for independently verifiable, facility-level methane emission data that operators can submit with confidence to third-party assurance providers. Fluxara's satellite attribution, combined with Bayesian uncertainty quantification, provides precisely the measurement-based, auditable emission data that assurance providers require to issue unqualified opinions on methane disclosures. This is the enterprise SaaS opportunity for Fluxara: large oil and gas operators in the EU, and their auditors and assurance providers (Deloitte, PwC, EY, KPMG), require a data product with the scientific credibility and independent verification documentation that Fluxara provides.

## 6. REGULATION 5 — US SEC CLIMATE DISCLOSURE RULES

### 6.1 Status as of Q2 2026

The SEC's climate disclosure rules, finalised in March 2024 under the Securities Exchange Act and Investment Advisers Act, were immediately challenged in litigation by the US Chamber of Commerce and multiple state attorneys general. In April 2025, the SEC under the new administration voluntarily stayed the rule pending judicial review. The rule's status remains uncertain as of June 2026; the SEC has indicated it is reconsidering its approach.

**Assessment for Fluxara:** The SEC rule created mandatory Scope 1 and 2 GHG disclosure requirements for large accelerated filers (Scope 3 disclosure was dropped in the final rule). If reinstated, it would apply to approximately 6,000 publicly listed US companies, including all major oil and gas operators. However, the rule is currently inoperative. Fluxara should monitor the SEC rulemaking but the SEC driver is a contingent rather than current demand signal.

## 7. REGULATION 6 — CALIFORNIA CLIMATE DISCLOSURE LAWS (SB 253 AND SB 261)

### 7.1 SB 253 — Climate Corporate Data Accountability Act

California Senate Bill 253 (Wiener, 2023), as amended by SB 219 (2024), requires US-organised companies with more than $1 billion in annual global revenue doing business in California to annually disclose their Scope 1, 2, and 3 greenhouse gas emissions in accordance with the GHG Protocol.

Scope 1 and 2 emissions disclosures are due by 10 August 2026. Scope 3 emissions reporting begins in 2027 for fiscal year 2026 data. SB 253 is unaffected by the Ninth Circuit injunction affecting SB 261 and remains fully in effect.

CARB may impose administrative penalties of up to $500,000 per year for SB 253 violations for failures including non-filing, late filing, or misstatements.

### 7.2 SB 261 — Climate-Related Financial Risk Act

SB 261 requires companies with more than $500 million in annual revenue doing business in California to publish biennial climate-related financial risk reports aligned with TCFD or equivalent frameworks. An injunction is in effect, and SB 261 reporting is currently voluntary pending the Ninth Circuit's decision. Oral argument took place in January 2026 and as of Q2 2026 a decision has not been issued.

### 7.3 How Fluxara Addresses These Laws

Because SB 253 applies to any company doing business in California — not just California-headquartered entities — it effectively creates nationwide GHG disclosure obligations for all major US operators with California revenue above $1 billion. All large US oil and gas companies (ExxonMobil, Chevron, ConocoPhillips, Pioneer, Occidental, Devon, Marathon, EOG) are in scope. SB 253's requirement for Scope 1 methane disclosure consistent with the GHG Protocol creates demand for the same high-quality, measurement-based, facility-level attribution data that Fluxara provides.

## 8. REGULATION 7 — EU EMISSIONS TRADING SYSTEM (EU ETS)

### 8.1 Scope and MRV Requirements

The EU Emissions Trading System (Directive 2003/87/EC, as amended by Directive (EU) 2023/959) covers major stationary emitters including upstream oil and gas refineries and LNG terminals. All covered entities must submit annual Monitoring Plans and Verified Emissions Reports aligned with the EU MRV Regulation (Implementing Regulation (EU) 2018/2066). Reports must be independently verified by accredited verifiers and submitted to the relevant national competent authority annually by 31 March.

Methane is not currently a covered gas under the EU ETS (which covers CO2, N2O, and perfluorocarbons). However, the EU Methane Regulation (Section 2 above) creates parallel methane-specific obligations that interface with the EU ETS monitoring infrastructure. The EU has signalled intent to review methane inclusion under Phase V of the EU ETS after 2030.

**Assessment for Fluxara:** The EU ETS MRV infrastructure, compliance culture, and accredited verifier ecosystem create a receptive regulatory environment for Fluxara's independent verification output. Oil and gas operators already operating under EU ETS compliance are structurally prepared to integrate additional satellite-verified methane reporting into their compliance workflows.

## 9. REGULATION 8 — CANADA ENHANCED METHANE REGULATIONS

### 9.1 Identity and Legal Basis

The Regulations Amending the Regulations Respecting Reduction in the Release of Methane and Certain Volatile Organic Compounds (Upstream Oil and Gas Sector) — the "Enhanced Methane Regulations" — were published in final form in the Canada Gazette, Part II on 31 December 2025, issued under section 93 of the Canadian Environmental Protection Act, 1999 (CEPA).

The oil and gas sector was responsible for 30% of Canada's GHG emissions in 2023, accounting for 208 megatonnes CO2e, making it the largest GHG emitter in Canada. The sector was also the largest source of methane emissions, accounting for about 47% of Canada's methane emissions.

### 9.2 Requirements and Timeline

The Amendments impose stricter restrictions on methane emissions from regulated oil and gas facilities, including restrictions on fugitive emissions management, prohibiting venting, and limiting hydrocarbon gas destruction, while also requiring regular inspections and maintenance. The Amendments will apply to new facilities as of 1 January 2028, and to all facilities by 2030.

The final regulations are projected to reduce methane emissions 72% by 2030 relative to 2012 levels. This provides Alberta and other provinces a longer runway to achieve the full 75% reduction target.

The regulations include mandatory LDAR (Leak Detection and Repair) programmes, prohibition on venting with narrow exceptions, and required regular inspections. Measurement-based verification of venting and fugitive emissions is mandated, creating direct demand for independent satellite-based attribution.

### 9.3 How Fluxara Addresses Canada's Regulations

Canada's Enhanced Methane Regulations create mandatory measurement-based compliance obligations for every significant upstream oil and gas facility in the country from 2028. The Alberta oil sands, BC Montney, and Permian Basin analogue producing regions in Saskatchewan represent a commercially significant market. Fluxara's TROPOMI-based pipeline covers Canada's producing basins within the same global data stream, requiring no separate satellite infrastructure.

## 10. REGULATION 9 — NORWAY: EEA INTEGRATION AND NATIONAL REQUIREMENTS

Norway is not an EU member state but is party to the European Economic Area (EEA) Agreement. The applicability of the EU Methane Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2024/1787) to Norway is subject to incorporation by the EEA Joint Committee. Applicability to the three EEA countries — Iceland, Liechtenstein, and Norway — will be subject to the EEA Joint Committee decision. Norway is the EU's largest single non-Russian gas supplier and a major LNG exporter; Norwegian continental shelf operators (Equinor, AkerBP, TotalEnergies Norway) are directly affected by the EU import MRV equivalence requirements and are expected to voluntarily comply with EU Methane Regulation standards as an EEA market access matter, irrespective of formal EEA incorporation.

Norway's existing domestic regulatory framework includes the Pollution Control Act, which requires Petroleum Safety Authority Norway (PSA) approval for all emissions from Norwegian Continental Shelf (NCS) operations, and the Climate Quota Act (Norway's national carbon pricing system, linked to the EU ETS from 2008). Norwegian operators are among the most technically advanced in the world for methane management and represent an early-adopter customer segment for Fluxara with sophisticated technical procurement processes.

## 11. REGULATION 10 — AUSTRALIA: NATIONAL GREENHOUSE AND ENERGY REPORTING (NGER) ACT 2007

Australia's National Greenhouse and Energy Reporting Act 2007 (NGER Act) requires companies meeting facility-level or corporate-level thresholds (25 kt CO2e at facility level, 50 kt CO2e at corporate level) to report annual greenhouse gas emissions and energy production to the Clean Energy Regulator. The oil and gas sector — including the Gorgon and North West Shelf LNG projects (Chevron), the Darwin LNG (Santos), and coal seam gas producers (Santos, Beach Energy) — is the largest emitter category in the NGER system.

The Safeguard Mechanism (effective 1 July 2023, reformed by the Safeguard Mechanism (Crediting) Amendment Bill 2023) requires facilities emitting more than 100 kt CO2e annually to hold baselines and surrender Australian Carbon Credit Units (ACCUs) for emissions above baseline. This creates direct financial incentive for accurate methane reporting, as underreporting carries reputational and regulatory risk while overreporting increases ACCU surrender obligations. Satellite-based independent verification of NGER-reported methane values is a commercial service with no current provider in the Australian market.

## 13. THE COMMON DEMAND SIGNAL

Every regulation in this landscape shares a single structural requirement: **measurement-based, independently verifiable, facility-level methane emission data**. The industry's historical reliance on engineering calculation-based reporting (GHGRP Subpart W, EU E-PRTR self-reporting) is explicitly targeted for replacement or supplementation by direct measurement in every one of the frameworks above.

The EU Methane Regulation mandates OGMP 2.0 Level 5, which requires direct measurement of source-level emissions — a standard that cannot be met by engineering estimates alone. The IRA WEC creates financial liability calibrated to GHGRP-reported values, creating incentive to report accurately (or face regulatory scrutiny if satellite data contradicts reported values). The CSRD requires independently assured Scope 1 data, and assurance providers increasingly require measurement-based evidence rather than calculation-based estimates for methane. California SB 253 requires GHG Protocol compliance, which mandates that Scope 1 methane be reported using the most accurate measurement methodology available.

Fluxara's satellite pipeline provides a continuous, independent, measurement-based verification layer applicable to all of these frameworks simultaneously. This is the product-market fit: one technical system addresses the verification gap across multiple regulatory regimes, for the same set of oil and gas operator customers who are in scope of all of them simultaneously.

## 14. REGULATORY RISK DISCLOSURE

### 14.1 US Political Risk

The current US administration has suspended the IRA Waste Emissions Charge and is reconsidering GHGRP Subpart W reporting requirements. Fluxara should not rely on US regulatory mandates as the primary commercial driver in 2026–2028. The EU and Canadian markets provide more robust near-term regulatory demand.

### 14.2 CSRD Omnibus Scope Reduction

The Omnibus I package reduced CSRD scope by approximately 80% relative to the original directive. Fluxara's target customer segment — large oil and gas operators — remains fully in scope (all have >1,000 employees and >€450m turnover), but the initial expectation of 50,000+ CSRD reporters is now approximately 10,000 larger companies. This does not affect Fluxara's core market but reduces the breadth of the secondary corporate buyer market.

### 14.3 EU Methane Regulation Implementation Infrastructure Gaps

The majority of Member States have not yet nominated Competent Authorities responsible for implementation and enforcement of the EU Methane Regulation. The EUMR lacks supporting infrastructure as various implementing acts, standards, methodologies, and resources are yet to be developed. This creates a window of regulatory uncertainty in 2025–2027 during which the precise verification methodology accepted by national competent authorities is not yet standardised. Fluxara should engage with the European Commission's OGMP 2.0 standardisation process to ensure Fluxara's output format is acceptable as verification evidence under the emerging implementing acts.

## 15. CONCLUSION

The regulatory landscape for methane MRV has never been more favourable for a product like Fluxara. Binding obligations now exist in the EU (in force since August 2024), Canada (effective 2028–2030), and at the sub-national level in California (first reporting August 2026), with the UNFCCC ETF creating ongoing national inventory verification needs globally. The temporary suspension of the US IRA Waste Emissions Charge does not eliminate the underlying statutory authority or the long-term trajectory toward direct methane pricing in the US. Fluxara's regulatory opportunity is concentrated in the EU and Canada in the 2026–2030 window, with the US market likely to reopen as political conditions evolve.

The total number of directly regulated facilities that require measurement-based methane MRV and could benefit from Fluxara's product is approximately 8,000 in the US (GHGRP Subpart W reporters), 2,500+ in the EU, and hundreds in Canada and Australia — before considering importers, financiers, auditors, and carbon market participants who require the same underlying data for their own compliance purposes.
